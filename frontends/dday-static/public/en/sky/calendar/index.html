    A year is not the same length in every calendar

    A purely lunar calendar runs 354 to 355 days, so its new year arrives about eleven days earlier each Gregorian year. One that keeps step with the seasons through a leap month runs anywhere from 353 to 385. Solar calendars run 365 to 366. The table below shows the range within the three years in the data, which does not reach either end of those rules.

    Nowruz is fixed by astronomy

    The Solar Hijri new year is the day of the vernal equinox if that moment falls before noon in Tehran, and the next day otherwise. This site computes the equinox from VSOP87D, so it can retrace that rule directly — over thirty-one years it never disagreed with the internationalisation tables, and the two branches of the rule fired sixteen and fifteen times.

    Our lunisolar calendar and the internationalisation tables agree

    This site computes the lunisolar calendar itself — new moons from Meeus chapter 49, solar terms from VSOP87D. The Dangi calendar in the internationalisation tables is an entirely separate implementation, and across the 37 months in the data not one month-start differs. Had either been wrong, it would have shown here.
