You satisfied R + W > N — so why is it still risky?

SymbolMeaning
NReplica count. How many machines hold one copy of the same piece of data. Cassandra calls it the replication factor, RF
WResponses a write waits for. Not "how many we write to" — the write goes to every replica, and W is how many acknowledgements you wait for before calling it a success
RResponses a read collects. How many replicas you gather answers from before picking one

With three replicas, R=W=2 guarantees overlap. But one dead node and writes fail. Drop to R=W=1 and you survive two failures — with a 33% chance of overlap. The inequality does not break on its own. It breaks the moment you lower the level to stay up.

Work the dials

DialWhat it changes
Replicas NHow many machines hold the piece. Three or five are common. QUORUM is computed from this — floor(N/2)+1.
Write waits for WHow many acks make a write a success. Raise it and you get safer while tolerating fewer dead nodes.
Read collects RHow many replicas a read gathers. Add it to W and exceed N and overlap is guaranteed.
Dead nodesReplicas that cannot answer. More dead than W or R and the operation itself fails — the point where talk of overlap becomes moot.

In the grid the write takes replicas from the front and the read from the back — the arrangement that minimises overlap. If they overlap in this layout they overlap in any layout. That is what the inequality asserts.

Things to try

1. N=3, R=W=2 — overlap is guaranteed

3 replicas · write waits for 2 · read collects 2 W+R = 4 > 3 → guaranteed · overlapping replicas 1 Failures tolerated 1

The most common setting (QUORUM/QUORUM). Four picks into three slots means the pigeonhole principle forces an overlap. The documentation puts it as "at least one of the replicas is guaranteed to participate in both the write and the read request." The amber cell in the grid is that replica.

2. Drop both to 1 — 33%

Write 2 → 1 · read 2 → 1 W+R = 2 ≤ 3 → no guarantee · overlap probability 33% Failures tolerated 1 → 2

The fastest and most survivable setting, and widely used. The price is that two times in three they do not overlap. That is not "usually fine" — it is 33%. (In a real system the write went to every replica anyway, so after a moment it is usually right. This probability is the value at the instant there is no guarantee.)

3. Kill one node — still holding

Back to R=W=2, dead nodes 1 Live replicas 2 · write succeeds · read succeeds

W is 2 and two are alive, so it fits exactly. The margin is precisely zero. This is R=W=2's limit — the meter had been reading "failures tolerated: 1" all along.

4. Kill two — writes fail

Dead nodes 1 → 2 (R=W=2 unchanged) Live replicas 1 · write fails · read fails

The inequality still says 4 > 3. And nothing can be done. You are waiting for two acks with one node able to answer. Guaranteeing overlap means "if it succeeds, they overlap" — it does not guarantee success.

5. Lower the level to stay up — this is the real path

With two dead, drop to R=W=1 Write succeeds · read succeeds · overlap probability 33%

Dropping QUORUM to ONE during an incident — this is how you actually end up reading a stale value. The inequality does not break by itself; we break it by hand. And that is usually the right call, since otherwise nothing works at all. The point is knowing what you gave up at that moment.

6. Same "guaranteed", different tolerance — R=1, W=3

Dead 0 · write 3 · read 1 W+R = 4 > 3 → guaranteed · yet failures tolerated 0

It satisfies the inequality and one dead node breaks writes. Look at the table below — inside the same "guaranteed" you have a 1-tolerance option (R=W=2) and a 0-tolerance one (R=1, W=3). Checking the inequality and stopping there hides that difference. The combination that tolerates the most while still guaranteeing is always floor((N−1)/2) — the majority quorum.

7. Go to N=5 — two failures and still guaranteed

5 replicas · R=W=3 (QUORUM) · dead 2 W+R = 6 > 5 → guaranteed · write succeeds · read succeeds

Adding replicas is the way out. QUORUM of five is three, so two can die and three remain, keeping the guarantee. At N=3 the limit was one. The price is node count and write latency — you now wait for three acks.

What the inequality actually buys

The documentation says: "This is typically expressed in the same terms Dynamo does, in that W + R > RF, where W is the write consistency level, R is the read consistency level, and RF is the replication factor." And when it holds, "at least one of the replicas is guaranteed to participate in both the write and the read request, which in turn guarantees that the quorums will overlap and the write will be visible to the read."

What it buys is overlap, and only that. Place W picks and R picks among N slots; if W+R > N the pigeonhole principle forces at least one shared. No more and no less.
What it does not guaranteeWhy
That the operation succeedsWaiting for W acks needs W nodes alive. Recipe 4 is that spot
That the overlapping node is recognised as newestEven with the new value present, something has to pick the newest among several answers. Implementations that pick by timestamp pick wrong when clocks skew
That other keys are safeQuorums are per partition. Holding for one key says nothing about another

The price is fault tolerance

Waiting for W acks means only N−W may die. Reads are the same at N−R, and surviving both takes the smaller of the two.

failures tolerated = min(N − W, N − R)
And the combination that maximises it while keeping W + R > N is always floor((N−1)/2) — the majority quorum.

This is where operational pressure comes from. At N=3 with two dead, QUORUM can do nothing. So somebody lowers the level to ONE — and that is the actual moment the inequality breaks. Not on its own, and not through some mechanism like sloppy quorum. A person breaks it, buying availability.

The cells the documentation does not fill

In the lab's grid, the green cells are what the documentation tells you. The rest are not in it — so they are filled by combinatorics.

The number of ways R reads can miss the write set entirely is C(N−W, R) out of C(N, R). So
P(overlap) = 1 − C(N−W, R) / C(N, R)
W · RSumP(overlap)
1 · 1 (ONE/ONE)233.3%
1 · 2 or 2 · 1366.7%
2 · 2 (QUORUM)4100% — guaranteed

At N=5 the ONE/ONE case gets worse, 20%. Adding replicas improves the tolerance of the guaranteed combinations and worsens the probability of the unguaranteed ones — they do not move together.

These probabilities are the value at the instant there is no guarantee. Real systems send the write to every replica and run repair behind it, so after a moment it is usually right — there is measurement work showing partial quorums are mostly consistent in practice (PBS, VLDB 2012), and those figures come from measured latency distributions, which is not the kind of thing this lab computes.

This edition's checkpoint

This topic was rejected once. The only primary source that measured quorum staleness used Monte Carlo simulation, which cannot be reproduced deterministically, and my hypothesis that "sloppy quorum breaks the inequality" contradicted the documentation (hints do not satisfy a consistency level).

It came back because the checkpoint was somewhere else. The documentation prints a table.

What the documentation givesWhat the model must reproduce
"QUORUM: A majority (n/2 + 1) of the replicas must respond."That floor(N/2)+1 comes out exactly, for N=1..7
"W + R > RF … guaranteed to participate in both"That the combinatorial probability is exactly 1 in those cells

In other words, the cells the documentation calls "guaranteed" and the cells where my combinatorics returns 100% have to coincide completely. Once they do, the remaining cells are trustworthy — and those remaining cells are what this page adds.

What this model assumes

AssumptionWhat breaks if it fails
The read set is drawn uniformlyThe probability assumes R replicas chosen at random. Real coordinators prefer nearby replicas, which biases the draw and moves the number
One partition onlyQuorums are per partition. An operation spanning keys holds per key, separately
The newest can be identifiedIt is assumed the overlapping node's value is recognised as newest. In practice a timestamp or vector clock does that, and if that mechanism is wrong, overlapping does not save you
Repair is not countedRead repair, hinted handoff and anti-entropy reconcile in the background. So this probability is the instant before any of that runs — it improves with time

In the field

WhereWhat to check
Find the levels in codeConsistency level is set per query. Find the default, and find where ONE is being used. The audit is usually surprising
Do not misread WW=1 is not "write to one node". The write goes to all replicas and only the number of acks awaited is 1. Which is why W=1 usually ends up correct — it just is not guaranteed
Incident runbooksIf "drop to ONE during an incident" is in the runbook, write down what that gives up too — and the condition for putting it back. Lowering it and forgetting is the common failure
Should N grow?It is the only way to gain tolerance while keeping the guarantee (3 → 5 takes you from 1 to 2). The price is storage and write latency — QUORUM goes from 2 to 3
Multiple datacentersLOCAL_QUORUM counts a majority within its own DC only. That is not a cross-DC guarantee — redo this page's arithmetic per DC
ClocksIf the newest is chosen by timestamp, clock skew becomes a correctness problem. You can overlap and still pick wrong

If no. 4 solves this problem with consensus, this page is the side that buys it with probability. And it differs from no. 15 too — that is isolation inside one node, this is overlap between nodes.
